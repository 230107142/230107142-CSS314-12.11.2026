### Forensic Explanation

The Apple M2 processor has a cache-line size of 128 bytes. Since each array element occupies 8 bytes, a stride of 16 provides 128-byte separation between worker slots.

The stride sensitivity experiment did not demonstrate a clear performance improvement at stride 16. The median execution time was 689.0416 ms at stride 1 and 698.4275 ms at stride 16.

However, the process scaling experiment showed better performance with stride 16 at eight processes. Its execution time was 708.2876 ms compared with 770.1861 ms at stride 1.

The Karp-Flatt serial fraction at eight processes was 0.1609 for stride 1 and 0.1271 for stride 16. This suggests that isolated memory locations provided a performance benefit in the scaling experiment, although the stride sweep did not establish a consistent cache-line effect.

### 2.2 Amdahl's Law Analysis

The measured serial fraction was:

s = 37.1951 / 3200.8538 = 0.011620

- Serial fraction: 1.162%
- Theoretical maximum speedup: 86.0558
- Theoretical speedup at 8 workers: 7.3982
- Observed speedup at 8 workers: 4.2929

The observed speedup was lower than the theoretical prediction. This difference can be explained by multiprocessing overhead, process communication, and other runtime limitations.

### 2.5 Phase 2 Conclusion

The experiment demonstrated that increasing the number of worker processes improved performance in both strong and weak scaling tests. However, efficiency decreased as the worker count increased. Strong scaling achieved a speedup of 4.2929 with 8 workers, while weak scaling achieved a Gustafson speedup of 7.6105.

The hardware has no additional logical CPUs beyond its 8 physical cores, so an SMT-related performance transition could not be measured.
